4 coordinates. One new perspective.

A little perspective.

Every point here has four coordinates: (x, y, z, w). You’re seeing a 3D projection of a 4D object, viewed through a 3D camera on your 2D screen. It’s a projection, not a cross-section.

The four sliders

The first three rotate the object in the XW, YW, and ZW planes, mixing a familiar axis with the fourth axis. The last rotates in XY. A 4D rotation happens in a plane, not around a single axis. These are four useful controls; 4D has six independent rotation planes.

Dragging the canvas orbits the resulting 3D projection. This is different from moving the sliders, which changes the 4D orientation itself. Touching a slider pauses animation so it stays under your control.

How the projection works

Perspective: (x, y, z) × d / (d − w)
Orthographic: (x, y, z)

Here d = 4.2. All objects are scaled to a 4D radius of 1.7, so the projection stays away from its singularity. Warm colors indicate positive rotated W; cool colors indicate negative W. The 3D camera uses perspective in both modes.
